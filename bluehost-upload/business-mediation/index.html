Civil and Business Mediator in Los Angeles

Business disputes cost more than legal fees. They drain time, attention, and relationships you may still need. Mediation gives the people involved a private, structured way to settle the dispute and get back to work.

Disputes Sean mediates

  • Partnership and shareholder disagreements, including business breakups
  • Contract disputes between companies, vendors, and clients
  • Landlord and tenant disputes over rent, repairs, deposits, and lease terms
  • Neighbor, association, and community disputes
  • Civil disputes before or during litigation

For conflict between employers and employees or within teams, see workplace mediation.

How business mediation works

The parties, often with their attorneys, meet with the mediator to clarify the dispute, share perspectives, and explore settlement options. Sessions frequently mix joint discussion with private caucuses, where each side can speak candidly with the mediator. If an agreement is reached, the key terms are written down so counsel can prepare final documents.

Why mediate instead of litigate

  • Privacy: court filings are public; mediation happens in a confidential setting
  • Control: the parties decide the outcome instead of a judge or jury
  • Speed and cost: many disputes resolve in a single, well-prepared session
  • Creative terms: payment plans, future business, and other options a court cannot order
  • Relationships: a better chance of preserving what can be preserved

Preparing for the session

Come with the key documents, a clear sense of what you need from a resolution and why, and someone with authority to settle. A short written summary for the mediator in advance helps the session move faster. Sean will tell you what is useful for your matter.

Questions about civil and business mediation

How long does business mediation take?

It depends on how many issues are in dispute, how complex the facts and documents are, and how prepared both sides are. Some matters settle in a single session; others take several sessions over a few weeks. There is no fixed timeline, and no one can promise one.

How much does business mediation cost?

Cost depends on the number of sessions and the preparation required. Mediation is often less expensive than contested litigation because it involves fewer hearings and less formal discovery, but every matter is different. Fees and payment terms are explained during the consultation.

What if we can’t reach a settlement?

Even without a full settlement, mediation often narrows the dispute and clarifies the real issues, which makes any remaining litigation shorter and cheaper. Litigation remains available for anything mediation does not resolve.

Can a business settlement be changed later?

A signed settlement is generally binding, so changes usually require both parties to agree in writing. That is why the terms are reviewed carefully, often by counsel, before anyone signs.
