Child Custody Mediator in Los Angeles

Your children need a plan that works on a Tuesday night, at Thanksgiving, and in the middle of summer. Custody mediation lets you and the other parent build that plan together, in private, with a neutral who keeps the conversation focused on the children rather than the conflict.

What a parenting plan covers

A good parenting plan answers the questions that cause friction before they come up. Sean works through each one with both parents, one decision at a time, until the plan is specific enough to follow without arguing over what it means.

Weekday and weekend schedule

Where the children sleep on school nights and weekends, and how the schedule fits each parent’s work and the children’s routines.

Holidays and special days

Thanksgiving, winter holidays, birthdays, Mother’s Day and Father’s Day, and religious or cultural celebrations: who has the children, and how years alternate.

School breaks and summer

Winter, spring, and summer breaks, vacations and travel, and how much notice each parent gives before taking the children away.

Decision-making

How you will decide together about education, health care, counseling, and activities, and what happens when you disagree.

Exchanges and transportation

Where and when handoffs happen, who drives, and how to keep exchanges calm and predictable for the children.

Communication

How you share information about school, health, and schedules, and how and when the children reach the other parent.

Co-parenting after the agreement

You will be parents together long after the paperwork is done. Mediation gives you practice making decisions as a team, and the plan you build reflects how your family actually lives. When the children grow, change schools, or pick up new activities, you can come back and adjust the plan rather than start a new fight.

Sessions can be held together or in separate rooms, in person or by Zoom. If being in the same room is hard, Sean can work with each parent separately and carry proposals between you.

Private mediation or court mediation

California family courts provide their own court-connected child custody services. Private mediation is separate, and some families use both.

  • Who decides: in private mediation, you and the other parent decide together. If parents cannot agree in court, a judge decides.
  • Privacy: private mediation happens in a confidential setting. Court proceedings are public.
  • Time and pace: private sessions are scheduled around your family, and you can take the time a detailed plan needs.
  • Tone: mediation is more private, more flexible, and less adversarial, which matters when you will keep raising children together.

Private mediation does not replace legal advice. Many parents consult their own attorneys before signing a final agreement. See how mediation works, or read about divorce mediation. For custody disputes that have become entrenched, see high-conflict settlement.

Questions about custody mediation

How long does custody mediation take?

It depends on how many issues are in dispute, how complex the schedule is, and how prepared both parents are. Some parents settle a parenting plan in a single session; others take several sessions over a few weeks. There is no fixed timeline, and no one can promise one.

How much does custody mediation cost?

Cost depends on the number of sessions and the preparation required. Mediation is often less expensive than contested litigation because it involves fewer hearings and less formal discovery, but every matter is different. Fees and payment terms are explained during the consultation.

What if we can’t agree on everything?

That is common, and it is not a failure. Most parents agree on more than they expect, and the remaining issues get smaller and clearer. Whatever you do agree on can be written down, and anything left unresolved can still go through the court process. Mediation does not take away your right to go to court.

Can a parenting plan be changed later?

Yes. Children grow, schools change, and schedules that worked at five rarely work at fifteen. Parents can come back to mediation to adjust an existing plan. If your plan is part of a court order, changes generally need court approval, so ask your attorney how that applies to you.
